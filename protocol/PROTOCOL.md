# noroshi study protocol

Working title: Nowhere to Report: Vulnerability Disclosure Channels at US Hospitals, Medical Device Manufacturers and EHR Developers.
Author: Christopher Jagdeo, Independent Researcher.
Status: FROZEN on 2026-11-17, before the main pass. The hash of the commit that froze this file is in `protocol/FROZEN.txt`, and the crawler refuses a full pass unless this file is identical to its version at that commit.

## 1. Objective

Measure, with a documented and reproducible method, whether US healthcare organizations publish any channel through which an outsider can report a security vulnerability.

## 2. Research questions

1. Across hospitals, medical device manufacturers, certified EHR developers and a federal control group, what share publish any channel: a `security.txt`, a policy page, a listing on a disclosure platform, or CVE Numbering Authority status?
2. On the same hospital websites, how does adoption of `security.txt` compare with adoption of `cms-hpt.txt`?
3. Among hospitals, which characteristics (type, ownership, size, rural location and system size) go with having a channel?
4. Where a channel exists, is it usable (contact type, expiry, safe-harbor promise on a policy page)?
5. When did adoption happen? This is a secondary question, answered by a supplementary analysis (section 6.8).

## 3. Prior work and the claim this study may make

Non-peer-reviewed figures exist and are cited by name: Future of Privacy Forum 2024 (12 of 2,282 hospitals with a valid `security.txt`, method not described); MedCrypt 2025 (27 of the "top 40" device manufacturers with a disclosure programme); CVD Portal 2026 (9 of 42 EU medical device makers); Internet Society 2019; Mandel 2014 (0 of 83 EHR vendors). Alkinoon et al. 2023 used the same hospital list with a breach linkage for other web measures.

The study claims to be, to the author's knowledge, the first peer-reviewed study to measure these channels across the three sectors with a documented method. It does not claim to be the first to look, or to have discovered that hospital adoption is low.

Yurcik et al. (Workshop on Cybersecurity in Healthcare, HealthSec at CCS 2024, doi:10.1145/3689942.3694754) mapped the internet presence of all US hospitals and measured shared IT infrastructure, market concentration and geographic distribution. Its abstract describes no measure of disclosure channels. The full text is closed access and was not read; the abstract was checked on 2026-11-16.

## 4. Populations (frames)

Unit of measurement: the registrable website domain, where both public and private suffixes count as suffixes (so `example.github.io` is its own domain). Registrable domains are computed with the public suffix list bundled with tldextract 5.4.0, private suffixes included, never updated over the network. All frames were fixed on 2026-11-16, before the crawler contacted any measured site. Two frame sources are themselves sites in the federal control group: the FDA device list (from `fda.gov`, fetched with a browser user agent because the site requires one) and the Lantern file (from `healthit.gov`). Once this protocol is frozen the frame commands refuse to run. The populations are hospitals with a listed website, makers of devices on FDA's list of AI-enabled medical devices (and, separately, vendors named in CISA medical advisories), developers of ONC-certified API technology, and a federal control group. None of them is a census of its sector.

### 4.7 Start URLs

The homepage is requested at `https://<host>/`. For hospitals the host is the one listed most often among the domain's hospitals (ties go to the shorter name, then alphabetical order), and the path of a listed URL is not used; for companies and federal domains it is the bare registrable domain. Plain http is never requested as a start URL, and TLS certificates are verified. When the request ends with a network error, one more request goes to the other host form (section 6.1). A site whose HTTPS request fails at the start URL and, when it is tried, at the other host form is unreachable. A redirect to plain http is followed.

## 5. Conduct

Requests:

- Only public files and pages are requested, with `GET`. No logins, forms or query manipulation. No vulnerability testing.
- A "site" is a registrable domain. All hosts of one site share its limits.
- In one pass, at most 12 requests are sent for any listed domain, counting retries and any request repeated after a crash.
- Independently, at most 12 requests other than homepage requests are made to any one site in a pass, across all listed domains; a request counts toward the site of the address it was made for, not toward the sites its redirects lead to. A homepage request (a listed domain's start URL, its other host form, the target of a homepage meta refresh, or the browser retry of any of them) is not counted in this limit and is never refused by it; it counts toward its own listed domain's 12.
- A request follows up to 5 redirects. Redirect hops are not counted as requests, and homepage requests are outside the site limit, so the study does not promise a fixed total per site: a site that other listed domains redirect to, or that the meta refresh of their homepages leads to, can receive more than 12 requests in a pass.
- One request at a time per site, at least 2.0 seconds apart. Every redirect hop obeys this too.
- A 60-second limit per request, summed over its redirect hops and counting only time between sending a hop and the end of its response; time spent waiting for a site's turn is not counted. A hop also fails when no connection is made within 10 seconds or no data arrives for 20 seconds. At most 4,194,304 bytes of a homepage response body and 1,048,576 bytes of any other response body (after decompression) are kept. Reading stops at the first chunk past that size, and the body is cut to it and flagged as truncated. Bodies of redirect and 429 responses are not read.
- Cookies are kept only across the redirect hops of one request and are never sent on a later request.
- URLs are compared, requested and stored in one spelling: scheme and host in lower case, no default port (`:443` for https, `:80` for http), no trailing dot on the host. The final address and the redirect hops of a response are stored as the site wrote them. A candidate, `Policy:` URL, conventional path or meta-refresh target is not requested when the same listed domain has already chosen that address for a request in the pass, whether or not that request could be sent (the intermediate hops of a redirect do not count), or has already had an answer that ended at it from a homepage, `Policy:`, candidate, conventional-path or meta-refresh-target request (a completed request, not refused, with status 200 or a 4xx; an answer to a `robots.txt`, `security.txt` or `cms-hpt.txt` request does not count), the addresses being compared in that spelling and without a fragment or a trailing slash. So a link to the homepage, or to a page an earlier page request's redirect ended at, is not requested again. A URL that an earlier request of the same pass, for any listed domain and any purpose, was made for and measured (the same address in that spelling, fragment and trailing slash included) is not requested again; the stored measurement is copied for the other listed domain and marked as reused.
- Every request to a measured site is written to the store before it is sent. A request left unfinished by a crash still counts toward every limit. A failure to write to the store stops the run.

Identification and refusals:

- Requests identify the study: `noroshi-research/1.0 (+https://github.com/k0nnect/noroshi)`. The linked page describes the study and how to opt out.
- When a site refuses the identified signature, the request is sent once more from its first address with a standard browser signature, unless the listed domain or the site has reached its request limit or the site has answered 429 meanwhile; then the refused attempt stands. A request whose last hop was sent with the browser signature is not retried. When the retry gets an answer (it completes, is not refused, and ends with status 200 or a 4xx that is not a refusal), the site that gave the refusal is switched: the rest of the requests to it in the pass use the browser signature. When the retry is refused too, fails or ends with any other status, the site stays on the identified signature, and a later refusal by it is retried the same way; each retry counts toward the limits. From the moment a site refuses the identified signature, while that request still holds the site's turn, until the browser retry of that request has settled (answered, refused, failed or not sent), no other request is sent to that site: a request whose turn comes then waits, at most 60 seconds for each such retry, and then takes its turn again and decides afresh (a stored answer of its address is reused, a 429 stops it, the limits hold, and the browser signature is used when the site was switched); a request that waited the full 60 seconds goes on without waiting for that retry again. The retry itself is not held. The retry exists because some sites refuse a request that identifies itself as a crawler and answer the same address normally to a browser: in the two pilots, 5 homepages answered the identified request with the same 52-byte `403 - Forbidden` page, and all 5 answered 200 to the browser retry. Without the retry such a site would be recorded as one that could not be measured, for a reason that says nothing about whether it publishes a channel. The signature is chosen at every redirect hop from the site of that hop (in a retry, hops at the refusing site use the browser signature), so the browser signature is never sent to a site that has not refused the identified one. The browser signature is the user agent of Safari 18.1 on macOS (`src/noroshi/config.py`); no other header changes.
- A refusal is a final response with status 401, 403 or 406, a `cf-mitigated` or `x-amzn-waf-action` header (any value, any status), or a challenge page. A challenge page is one whose title begins `Just a moment...`, or whose first 32 KiB of body hold one of seven fixed strings of `src/noroshi/blocking.py` (on any status, compared without regard to case): `attention required! | cloudflare`; Akamai's error address `errors.edgesuite.net`, and the same address with HTML entities as Akamai writes it (`errors&#46;edgesuite&#46;net`); `incapsula incident id`; `px-captcha`; `pardon our interruption`; `spamfirewall` (CleanTalk's challenge page). Incapsula's script address `_incapsula_resource` in the first 32 KiB of the body also makes a challenge page, but only when the status is 200 and the page has fewer than 200 visible characters (text outside `title`, `script` and `style` elements, whitespace collapsed).
- A 429 is never retried within the pass. After a 429 from a site, nothing more is sent to that site in the pass, by any listed domain. A listed domain ends the pass as blocked whenever its own site, its start URL's site, the site its homepage landed on or the site a homepage meta refresh led to answers 429 in the pass: before its crawl begins, at its homepage, or at any later request, even after its homepage had loaded. A 429 from any other site only skips that page.

robots.txt:

- The homepage is requested first, without regard to robots.txt, as is every other homepage request (the other host form and the target of a homepage meta refresh). robots.txt is then read from the host the homepage ended on, and it governs the `Policy:` URL, candidate pages, conventional paths and the meta-refresh targets of those pages on that host only. The agent name matched is `noroshi-research`. The homepage goes first because the host whose robots.txt applies is known only once the homepage has been requested.
- A robots.txt answered with status 200 and not refused is obeyed. A 4xx that is not a refusal means everything is allowed. Anything else (a 5xx, a network error, a refusal such as 401, 403 or 406 or a challenge page, any other status) means no page on that host is requested. Treating a refused robots.txt as disallow-all is stricter than RFC 9309, which reads 401 and 403 as allow.
- `security.txt` and `cms-hpt.txt` exist to be read by third parties and are requested regardless.
- A candidate page, `Policy:` URL or page meta-refresh target on any other host, including another host of the same site, is requested without reading that host's robots.txt, because reading it would cost a further request to a host the study did not list.

Opting out:

- From the start of the first run after it is added to `info/optout.txt`, an opted-out domain is never requested, including as a redirect target or the target of a meta refresh. A request whose redirect leads to an opted-out domain is stopped before that hop and counts as failed; its record holds no address of the opted-out site. A homepage stopped this way, or whose meta refresh leads to an opted-out host (nothing is sent there), ends the domain `optout` (section 6.3). An entry covers the host given and its whole registrable domain.
- A third-party page that belongs to an opted-out domain is skipped; the domain that linked to it is still measured. This also holds when the opt-out arrives after collection: the page is deleted, is not judged, and counts as never requested.
- A domain that opts out after collection gets status `optout`: its pages are not judged, it is not asked about in the identity check (section 6.3) and it is in no outcome. `noroshi purge` works on the registrable domain of the entry however it is written (with or without `www.`, in any case, with a trailing dot), on one store and its files per command: `noroshi purge DOMAIN` on the main store and `noroshi purge DOMAIN --store pilot` on the second pilot's store. An opted-out domain is purged from both. It deletes the request records of the listed domain; the page copies no other record uses; its judgements and those of the pages whose records it removes; its identity decisions; its Internet Archive rows, and those another listed domain's file looked up at a host of the site; the mark that switched its site to the browser signature; the model-call log entries of the judge about a page whose requested or final address is on the site, whichever listed domain asked about it; and the model-call log entries of its identity check. A judge entry that stays, because its page is on another site, has the opted-out domain's name taken out of it. It appends the date and the counts of what was removed to `data/raw/purge.log` (`data/raw/pilot_purge.log` for the pilot store).
- A purge also deletes every record of any other listed domain that names the site: requested there, redirected through it, ended on it, failed at it, or naming it in its error text. A domain whose homepage record is removed this way becomes opted out (its identity decision and the model-call entries of its identity check are deleted too), a file or page another domain read there counts as never requested, and a planned page of a look whose record a purge removed makes that look incomplete, as a skipped page does (section 6.6). What a resumed pass needs of the removed records is kept as counts that hold no address of the site: the requests each other listed domain and each other site had received in the pass, and each other site that had answered 429. So the limits and the 429 stop of this section hold across a purge.
- A purge is one transaction on the store, with the deleted content overwritten, followed by steps that can each be repeated. A purge stopped at any point is finished by running `noroshi purge` again, and until then `noroshi crawl`, `judge`, `identity`, `derive` and `analyze` do not run on that store. A purge does not run while a crawl, a judge run or an identity run holds the lock of either store (the main store and the pilot store share the model-call log), and none of them runs while a purge does. `noroshi analyze` also refuses while the store still holds records of an opted-out site, and while the purge log is newer than the derived tables. After a purge of both stores and of the archived pilot copies, no request record, log, decision or result written by the study's commands holds an address of the opted-out site, except the opt-out list; the purge log's line for it; its own row in the frame tables and in the per-domain table (marked `optout`); the logged resolution of company websites (section 4.5) that produced a company's row; another listed domain's Internet Archive row that names the site as a redirect target, and the value derived from it (a fact about that other domain); copies of other sites' pages that mention it, which are not edited; and this protocol, which names pilot domains in sections 12 and 13. The archived copies of the two pilot rounds (the first pilot's files under `data/raw/pilot1/` and any other set-aside pilot copies, such as `data/raw/pilot2-before-fix3/`), which no command reaches, are purged the same way or deleted, and the terminal output of a run is not kept.

Running:

- The crawler sends no request unless the file `info/LIVE` exists and names the information page's URL. The author creates it once the page is live, and a run stops if the file is removed.
- Only one of `noroshi crawl`, `judge`, `identity` and `purge` runs on a store at a time (a lock file beside the store); a second one stops at once with a message naming the first.
- Run with `--sector all`, sectors go in a fixed order: hospitals, federal, device (FDA list), device (ICSMA), EHR. A domain that appears in two tables is crawled once per pass, under the first of its sectors to run and with that sector's rules (section 6.1 step 5, section 6.3: whether `cms-hpt.txt` is requested and the third-party list), and the same crawl is reported in each sector.
- If 20 domains in a row (in the order their homepage steps finish, across up to 20 domains crawled at once and across sectors) end their homepage step with a network error (DNS, connection, TLS or timeout), and a control request to the study's own information page also fails (no response, or status 400 or more), the run stops. A homepage step ends with a network error when the start URL fails and no other host form is tried, when the other host form fails, or when the target of a homepage meta refresh fails (although the page that refreshed had answered); a failed first form that is followed by the other host form does not count on its own. A sent homepage request that ends in anything else starts the count again, and so does a control request that loads; a domain whose homepage comes from a stored or reused record neither adds to the count nor interrupts it. The control request is noted in the run log.
- At the stop, the counted domains, and any domain whose first host form had failed while its other form was still being requested, lose their done mark for the pass and their failed homepage requests are marked as left by the outage, never an outcome. Those domains are crawled again when the pass is run again and are not recorded as unreachable. Their failed homepage requests still count toward their limit for the pass. A homepage network error that lands after the stop is treated the same way. A domain that had finished and was not among those counted is not touched by the stop. On the rerun a domain whose other host form had already answered keeps that answer as its homepage and its first form is not sent again; a request that completed earlier in the pass is not sent again, except a refused identified attempt whose browser retry never completed, which is sent again with its retry.
- Two pilots are measured in pass 1 only, before this protocol is frozen, into a separate store. Their data is not used in the results, and their domains are measured again in the main pass. The first pilot drew 30 domains per sector at random with a recorded seed (150 sector rows) and ran on 2026-11-16. The second pilot drew its sample from each sector table after the first pilot's domains were removed from it (`noroshi crawl --limit N --seed S --exclude FILE`, where the file lists the first pilot's websites one per line; a domain in two tables is removed from both; it takes every domain that remains when fewer than N do). It measured 150 sector rows on 148 distinct domains and ran on 2026-11-17 between 00:15 and 00:23 UTC. The changes made after each pilot are listed in section 12.

Other:

- Contacts found in `security.txt` files are never tested and never released.
- The unit is the organization. No human participants are involved, no personal data are collected for the analysis, and no institution takes part, so no ethics review was sought. Stored copies of public files can contain contact addresses; the point above covers them. The study follows the principles of the Menlo Report; the measures of this section (public files and pages only, low volume, requests that identify the study, an opt-out with deletion, no test of any contact) are how.

## 6. Measurement

### 6.1 Request order per domain

1. Homepage (the frame's start URL; see section 4.7). When it ends with a network error (DNS, connection, TLS or timeout), one more homepage request goes to the other host form (`www.` added when the host has none, removed when it has one; same scheme, path `/`), unless any homepage attempt at the start URL in this pass was refused, the other form was already a redirect hop of the first attempt, or the first attempt failed at the other form's host; the response of the other form is then the homepage. When the homepage answers 200 (and is not refused) with an HTML page that has fewer than 200 visible characters and a `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` tag (the first such tag) whose delay, read as a number, is 5 seconds or less, the target of that tag, resolved against the page's final address, is requested once and its response is the homepage; a target equal to the page's own address, or already requested (section 5), is not requested, and the target's own refresh is not followed. Steps 2 to 5 and 8 go to the scheme and host of the homepage's final URL after redirects, written in one spelling (section 5); the `Policy:` URL (step 6) and candidate links (step 7) are requested at the address they give, which may be another host or another site.
2. `/robots.txt`.
3. `/.well-known/security.txt`.
4. `/security.txt`, unless step 3's file was classified valid or valid with problems (section 6.4).
5. `/cms-hpt.txt` (hospitals only).
6. The first `Policy:` URL of the well-known file or, if it has none, of `/security.txt`, from any file read as text (classified invalid, valid with problems or valid). It is requested only when it is an http or https address, not on a disclosure platform, not already requested (section 5), and, on the final host, allowed by robots.txt.
7. Candidate pages: up to three links from the homepage (two when a `Policy:` URL met the conditions of step 6, even if its request could then not be sent because its site had opted out, had used its limit or had answered 429): the links of the specific tier in document order, then those of the general tier in document order, each address once (addresses that differ only in the case of the scheme or host, a default port, a trailing dot on the host, a fragment or a trailing slash are one address). A link already requested (section 5) or disallowed by robots.txt keeps its place and is not replaced by a later one.
8. Up to 6 conventional paths, in order: `/vulnerability-disclosure-policy`, `/responsible-disclosure`, `/security`, `/vulnerability-disclosure`, `/product-security`, `/psirt`.

A `Policy:` URL, candidate or conventional-path page that answers 200 with such a meta refresh (an HTML page of fewer than 200 visible characters, delay of 5 seconds or less) is followed by one request to its target with the same purpose, unless the target was already requested (section 5). On the final host the request is made only when robots.txt allows it; a target on another host is requested without reading that host's robots.txt. The target stands in for the page, its own refresh is not followed, and it counts in the limits of section 5 like any other non-homepage request. Links on a `Policy:`, candidate or conventional-path page are not followed; the meta refresh is the only step past such a page.

Candidate links are the http or https targets of `<a href>` elements in the homepage as served (no script is run; only the first 4,194,304 bytes), resolved against the address in the page's first `<base href>` when that gives an http or https address (a relative one is resolved against the page's final URL), otherwise against its final URL. A link is in the specific tier when its anchor text or decoded address matches, ignoring case: vulnerability disclosure; responsible disclosure; coordinated (vulnerability) disclosure; coordinated vulnerability; vulnerability or vulnerabilities report; vulnerability or vulnerabilities reward(s); report (a) (security) vulnerability; report (a) security issue, bug, flaw or concern; bug bounty; security researcher(s); product security; PSIRT; or when its decoded address contains `/vulnerability-disclosure`, `/responsible-disclosure`, `/product-security`, `/psirt`, `/bug-bounty`, `/vulnerabilit`, `/coordinated-disclosure` or `/security-disclosure`. Any other link is in the general tier when its anchor text, trimmed, has at most 5 words and at most 40 characters and contains, as whole words in any case, `security`, `cybersecurity`, `cyber security`, or `trust` followed by `center`, `centre`, `hub` or `portal`, and does not contain `social security`; or when the whole path of its address is `/security`, `/cybersecurity`, `/cyber-security`, `/trust` or `/trust-center`, with or without a trailing slash or `.html` (`src/noroshi/discover.py`). Links to a disclosure platform (HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti, YesWeHack or any subdomain of them) are never requested, and neither is a `Policy:` URL on one; a requested page whose redirect or meta refresh leads to a platform is followed like any other. A platform link with a non-empty path can count as a listing (section 6.7). Sections 6.6 and 6.7 use a separate list for the text of a page or link, the page pattern list. It has the wording of the specific tier's text patterns and none of the general tier's: vulnerability disclosure; responsible disclosure; coordinated (vulnerability) disclosure; coordinated vulnerability; vulnerability or vulnerabilities report; vulnerability or vulnerabilities reward(s); report (a) (security) vulnerability; report (a) security issue, bug, flaw or concern; bug bounty; security researcher(s); product security; PSIRT (`PAGE_TEXT_PATTERN` in `src/noroshi/discover.py`). The address patterns of the specific tier and the general tier do not widen it.

Files are requested with the scheme of the homepage's final URL; a `security.txt` whose final address is not https is invalid. When the listed domain's limit, or the site limit of one of its own sites (the listed domain's site, the start URL's site, the site the homepage ended on, or the site of a homepage meta-refresh target), is reached, the remaining steps are not made; when another site's limit is reached, only the request to that site is not made (section 6.6). Because the order is fixed, conventional paths are the steps most often cut, and browser-signature retries draw on the same limits.

### 6.2 Passes

Pass 1 covers every domain. In pass 2 a domain is crawled again in full (section 6.1) under fresh limits, no earlier than 48 hours after the first request recorded for it in pass 1 (a domain with no pass-1 request, such as one blocked before anything was sent, has no wait). The retry pass is started no earlier than 48 hours after the main pass ended, so that no site is requested again within 48 hours of any pass-1 request to it. There is one retry pass and no third pass, and the main pass and the retry pass are each run once to the end (a stopped run is resumed, not repeated). The collection window, the dates of the first and last request of each pass, is read from the store by hand and reported. A domain whose crawl failed is run again in its pass. The main analysis refuses to run while any domain is uncrawled or any measured domain has no identity decision for the homepage it was measured from (section 6.3), unless the author gives the flag `--allow-incomplete`, which prints how many of each it lets through; the reported results are produced without it. It also refuses, with no override, in the cases of section 5: a purge stopped before it finished, records of an opted-out site still in the store, and a purge log newer than the derived tables. Pass 2 covers only domains that were blocked or unreachable, or whose homepage, `security.txt` or `cms-hpt.txt` request could not be completed, or whose `security.txt` or `cms-hpt.txt` request that the order of section 6.1 called for was never made (for example because a request limit was reached first; a file classified forbidden counts as completed), or whose look for a policy page could not be completed because robots.txt could not be read or a page request failed.

A request counts as a measurement only when it completed, was not refused, and ended with status 200 (the thing was read) or a 4xx that is not a refusal (the thing is not there). Any other ending means the request could not be measured.

Combining passes:

- The look for a policy page is judged on both passes together, over the passes that measured the domain. robots.txt counts as unreadable only when none of them read it, and as disallowing every conventional path only when every one that read it does. A skipped, disallowed or failed page is cleared when any record in either pass is a measurement of that address, or is a page request (section 5) that ended at it with an answer. The homepage counts as cut only when every one of them cut it, and as unreadable (fewer than 200 visible characters) only when every one of them found it so. A conventional path counts as cut only when no pass requested it or ended a page request at its address with an answer.
- For each file (`security.txt` at each location, `cms-hpt.txt`), the most recent result across both passes is used, except as in the last bullet; a result is a measurement or a file classified forbidden (section 6.4), and a later failed attempt never replaces an earlier result. File records count from either pass, including a pass that ended blocked after reading them. The homepage is the one the latest pass that measured the domain was measured from: that pass's last homepage request that was an outcome (not cancelled, left in flight or relabelled by an outage), so that after a network error and the other host form, or after a followed meta refresh, it is the address that answered.
- A domain is opted out when either pass ended optout or it matches the opt-out list; otherwise it is measured when either pass measured it; otherwise it has the status of the latest pass that ran. Its final site, its homepage links and its listings are read from that homepage, never from a pass that ended blocked, unreachable or mismatch.
- A `security.txt` location found valid or valid with problems, or a `cms-hpt.txt` found present and valid, in either pass stands, even if the other pass found it absent. Domains whose passes disagree (the last result of a file in pass 1 and in pass 2, a measurement or a forbidden file, fall in different classes) are flagged in the per-domain table and counted in the flow table.

### 6.3 Domain status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| measured | Homepage answered 200, was not refused, passed the three crawl tests below, no site of the domain answered 429 before its crawl ended, and the identity check below found the page to be the organization's working website (domains listed only in the federal table are exempt from that check) |
| blocked | Homepage refused, including after the browser-signature retry when one was sent; or the listed domain's site, its start URL's site, its homepage's final site or the site of a homepage meta refresh target answered 429 in this pass, even after the homepage loaded; or a homepage redirect or meta refresh led into a site that had |
| unreachable | Homepage failed with a network error (DNS, connection, TLS, timeout) at the start URL and, when it was tried, at the other host form, or at the target of a meta refresh, or failed with more than 5 redirects, a redirect no request can be built from, or a decoding error, or ended with any status that is not 200 and not a refusal (for example 404, 503 or 204) |
| mismatch | Homepage loaded but is not the organization's own working website: one of the three crawl tests below, or the identity check below (the per-domain table records `crawl_rule` for any of the three crawl tests, without saying which, or `identity_other` or `identity_unclear`) |
| optout | The listed domain is opted out, or its homepage was requested at, redirected through, led by a meta refresh to or landed on an opted-out host in either pass |

The crawl ends a domain mismatch when any of these holds:

- Its final site is a domain sale or parking service, or a third-party platform (the list in `src/noroshi/domains.py`; federal portals count as third parties for hospitals only).
- Its title or visible text carries a for-sale or parked-domain marker (`domain is for sale`, `this domain is for sale`, `buy this domain`, `domain for sale`, `this domain is parked`, `domain parking`, without regard to case).
- A homepage request that started on one host ended, after redirects, on a host `ww<digits>.<listed domain>` (a parking service's host) with a response of any status, or a followed homepage meta refresh pointed to such a host. Every homepage request of the pass counts, a refused one and a browser retry included, and this is checked before refusal and failure; a request that only passed through such a host, or failed there, does not count. A start host of that form is not a mismatch by itself.

The crawl does not compare a homepage with the organization's name: a homepage that answers 200, is not refused and passes these three tests is measured by the crawl, and the identity check below can still end it as a mismatch. Name words (used in section 6.7) are the lowercase words of four or more letters in the frame names of the organizations on the domain in that sector's table (a domain in two tables uses each table's names in that table's row; federal rows have none), less a fixed stopword list (`src/noroshi/frames/__init__.py`).

Identity check. After the crawl, two models decide for every listed domain that a pass measured (once, even when the domain is in several tables) whether the stored homepage it was measured from, in its latest measured pass, is the organization's own working website. Domains listed only in the federal table are not checked, because the .gov registry controls those names, and opted-out domains are not checked. The raters are `claude-sonnet-5-5` and `claude-haiku-5-5`, each asked without the other's answer and without tools, with a fixed prompt (`IDENTITY_MODELS` in `src/noroshi/config.py`, `PROMPT` in `src/noroshi/identity.py`). The prompt gives the organization's listed names (for hospitals, up to five hospitals on the domain, largest by beds first, with the number of further hospitals at the domain and the cities and states of those named; for companies, every company name that resolved to the domain; a domain in several tables gets the names of all of them), the listed domain, the host the homepage ended on, the page title (the first `title` element outside an inline `svg` or `math` element), the `og:site_name` and `description` meta tags (the title and each meta value cut to 300 characters), the first 2,000 characters of the homepage's visible text (text outside `title`, `script` and `style`, whitespace collapsed), and its last 800 characters that are not among those 2,000. It asks whose website the page is and allows three answers: `own` (the listed organization's own working website, or the working website of the organization that now owns or operates it or answers for its products: a parent company, an acquirer, a successor or a health system; a site in another language counts when it is the same organization; a page that shows little text of its own, for example one that needs scripts to display or a sign-in page, counts when its title or meta tags name that organization or its product); `other` (anything else: an unrelated organization or business, a parked or for-sale domain, a placeholder or hosting default page, an error page, a bot check or access-denied page, or a site reusing the name for unrelated content); `unclear` (nothing on the page, its title, its meta tags or its text, shows whose website it is). Each rater also gives a one-sentence reason. A rater, or the tie-break model, is asked once more when its call fails or its reply cannot be read as one of the three words. A rater or the tie-break model that gives a readable reply on either try is counted with that reply. One whose two replies both could not be read counts as `unclear`, marked `reply_unreadable`, and the decision goes on by the usual rule, so a tie-break counted that way decides `unclear`; one that gave no readable reply because a try was a failed call leaves the domain undecided, and it is asked again on a later run. When the two raters differ, `claude-opus-5-5` decides alone. Every call is logged. A SIGINT, SIGTERM or SIGHUP stops the run: the calls in flight finish and are logged and complete decisions are written, and no new call starts, not even the next rater, a retry or the tie-break of a domain in progress, which stays undecided and is asked again on a later run; the command then exits with an error. A model process runs in its own session, so a terminal's interrupt does not cut a call short. A homepage decided `other` or `unclear` makes the domain a mismatch (`identity_other` or `identity_unclear` in the per-domain table), with the empty outcomes of any mismatch. A decision holds only for what the models were shown (the host, the title, the meta values and the two pieces of text): when the homepage a domain was measured from shows them something different, for example after pass 2, the earlier decision does not count and the domain is asked again, and a view already decided is not asked again. The analysis does not run until every measured domain has a decision (section 6.2). The two raters' agreement and Cohen's kappa are reported (section 8), with the number of answers, by a rater or the tie-break model, recorded as `unclear` because their replies could not be read.

Pages are read as UTF-8 whatever charset they declare. A homepage that redirects to another registrable domain is measured there (for example an acquirer or a health system) unless the identity check finds that page is not the organization's working website, and a measured domain whose homepage ended on another registrable domain is flagged as redirected off-domain in the per-domain table. The number of such measured domains is reported in the flow table. A homepage meta refresh to another registrable domain is followed the same way (section 6.1): the domain is measured there and flagged on the same terms.

Blocked, unreachable and mismatch domains are "could not measure". They are left out of the primary shares; only the sensitivity analysis (section 8) counts them as lacking a channel. Hospital domains whose final status is unreachable are looked up in the Internet Archive. When the most recent capture of the start host's homepage is a redirect to another registrable domain, that domain is recorded in the per-domain table. Those domains are not measured at the new address. The number of such domains is reported in the hospital row of the flow table.

### 6.4 Classifying `security.txt`

Each location is classified in order: the file's identified attempt and its browser retry, at the same address, both ended with status 401 or 403, both answers are plain (neither is a challenge page (section 5), and neither body contains `<script`, in any case, anywhere in it), and the domain's homepage in the same pass was answered with status 200 and not refused → forbidden (nobody outside can read the file; it is not retried in pass 2); refused, failed, or any final status that is neither 200 nor a 4xx → unmeasured (this includes a refusal whose browser retry could not be sent, and a 401 or 403 pair in which either answer is a challenge page or contains a script); a 4xx that is not a refusal → absent; 200 with an HTML page → HTML error page (absent); 200 text with no valid contact, or not served over HTTPS → invalid; valid contact but no valid unexpired Expires field → valid with problems; otherwise valid. A response is an HTML page when its content type contains `html` or its body begins with an `html`, `head`, `body` or doctype tag. Any other 200 response is parsed as `security.txt` whatever its content type; whether it was served as `text/plain`, and whether its final address is on a registrable domain other than the domain's final site, are reported. Expiry is judged at the time the request was sent. A file requested from a site that was already switched to the browser signature in the pass gets one attempt, with that signature, so it is never classed forbidden: a 401 or 403 there is a refusal and leaves the file unmeasured.

The domain's class combines the two locations: a valid file wins over a valid-with-problems file wherever it is, with the well-known location breaking a tie; otherwise, if either location is unmeasured (including a location never requested), the domain is unmeasured; otherwise invalid, then HTML error page, then forbidden, then absent. A forbidden class counts as no file, as absent does, for the main and strict outcomes and for the paired comparison. Whether any of a measured domain's files (either `security.txt` location, or `cms-hpt.txt`) is forbidden is recorded per domain and counted in the flow table.

A contact is valid when it is `mailto:` followed by an address with a dotted domain and no whitespace, `https://` followed by a dotted host with no whitespace, or `tel:` followed by a value containing a digit. A bare email address or an `http:` URL is recorded as lenient and does not count. Expires must be a single date-time of the form `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ss`, with optional fractions and a `Z` or `+hh:mm`/`-hh:mm` offset, later than the time the request was sent. A value without an offset is invalid, as in RFC 3339. A space may replace the `T`, and letters are read in either case. PGP-signed files are parsed inside the wrapper; signatures are not verified. A second parser (the `sectxt` library, version 0.9.8) is run on the file chosen for each measured domain whose class is valid, valid with problems or invalid. It is compared on one point only: whether the file has a valid contact. Agreement is recorded per domain and counted among main-outcome domains in the quality table. The study's own parser decides every class. Disagreements are listed in `results/parser_disagreements.csv` (every measured domain whose two parsers disagree, with the chosen file's address, class and contact types and both verdicts), each is examined against RFC 9116, and any resulting change is reported.

### 6.5 Classifying `cms-hpt.txt`

Present and valid when the file is read as text (as for `security.txt`: not refused, status 200, not HTML) and has at least one `mrf-url:` line whose value begins `http://` or `https://` and one `location-name:` line with a value (field names from CMS's own generator). A text file without both is present but invalid. The rule (45 CFR 180.50(d)(6)) places the file at the root of whichever site hosts the machine-readable price file, which may be a health-system domain, so absence on a hospital's domain is not proof of non-compliance. A `cms-hpt.txt` refused with 401 or 403 to both signatures, both answers plain (section 6.4), while the homepage answered 200, is forbidden (no file), as in section 6.4. A present file whose final address is on a registrable domain other than the domain's final site is flagged in the per-domain table.

### 6.6 Policy pages

Judging. Every stored candidate, conventional-path and policy-field page with status 200 is examined. Before the pattern list or any model, a page is set aside, in this order: a candidate or conventional-path page whose final address is the final address of its listed domain's homepage in that pass (same key), whatever its size or whether it was cut, because it is the homepage (`same_as_homepage`); a PDF cut at the size limit, a PDF from which `pdftotext` gets no text, and a body that is not text (`pdf_unreadable`, `non_text`), except that a page answered with an image (`image/*`) is set aside as `image`; a page that answered with a meta refresh (section 6.1) whose target was requested for the same listed domain in the same pass, or is the final address of an answered homepage, `Policy:`, candidate or conventional-path request of that domain and pass, because that record stands in for it (`meta_refresh`); a page with fewer than 200 visible characters (text outside `title`, `script` and `style`; for a PDF, the text `pdftotext` gives), or one cut at the size limit in which nothing in the part kept matches the page pattern list or mentions `security.txt` (`unreadable`); and a conventional-path page when another conventional-path page of the same listed domain and pass, with a different final address, has the same visible text, because the site answers any path with one page (`catch_all`). A catch-all page, a refresh page, an image and a page that is the homepage count as read and are not policy pages; the other kinds count as unread (below). A page whose `pdftotext` was ended by a signal after the run was asked to stop gets no row and is read again on a later run; a `pdftotext` that ended by a signal on its own (a malformed PDF) makes the page `pdf_unreadable`. A page whose visible text matches nothing in the page pattern list (section 6.1) and does not mention `security.txt` is a clear negative and is not sent to a model. Every other page is judged independently by `claude-opus-5-5` and `claude-sonnet-5-5` with a fixed prompt (in `src/noroshi/judge.py`) of three yes/no questions about what the page itself says: does it tell outsiders how to report a vulnerability in the organization's own websites, systems or products (a page that points reporters to a reporting address or a disclosure platform counts); does it give a way to send such a report; does it commit not to pursue legal action against people who report in good faith. The first question excludes a page whose only relevant content is a navigation or footer link to a policy elsewhere, a general home, navigation or error page, and pages about patient privacy complaints, compliance or fraud hotlines, phishing emails someone received, or physical security; the second accepts an email address, web form, phone number or link to a disclosure platform. The prompt tells the models to answer no to the second and third questions whenever the first answer is no, shows no example answer, and does not contain the page's address. Each call is retried once if it fails or its reply cannot be read; pages with identical visible text are judged once, whatever their bytes and whichever domain or pass they come from. The models are run without tools. A disagreement on either of the first two is settled by `claude-fable-5-2`, which answers the same questions, and the majority of the three answers decides each; a disagreement on the third is settled the same way when the page is a policy page, and the third answer is no for any other page. A page is a policy page when the final answer to both of the first two is yes. The judges' agreement and Cohen's kappa are reported (section 8). The answers of both judges, the tie-break answer and whether the two judges agreed are stored for every judged page in `data/raw/judgements.jsonl`; each row also holds a hash of the page's visible text unless the page was set aside as `same_as_homepage`, `pdf_unreadable`, `non_text` or `image`, and a page set aside holds its reason. A SIGINT, SIGTERM or SIGHUP stops the run as it stops the identity check (section 6.3), with one difference: no judgement row is written once the signal has arrived, so the page whose calls were in flight is judged again on the next run; the command then exits with an error, also when the signal came during its last page.

What the models see. The page's visible text (text outside `script`, `style` and `noscript`, whitespace collapsed), not its address, whole when it is 40,000 characters or less. For a longer page they see the first 20,000 characters, then 1,000 characters on each side of every pattern match, email address, `mailto:`, disclosure-platform host name and mention of `security.txt`, merged where they touch and in page order, up to 40,000 characters including separators. A body beginning `%PDF` is converted with `pdftotext`; a PDF cut at the size limit, or one from which `pdftotext` gets no text, is unreadable. Pages are read as UTF-8 whatever charset they declare. The requested address, the final address and the listed domain are written beside each call in the model-call log; they are not given to the models.

The domain's policy-page value has three levels:

- yes: at least one judged page is a policy page. If every policy page found is on a registrable domain other than the final site, the domain is flagged.
- unmeasured: no policy page was found and the look was incomplete. The reason is recorded: robots.txt could not be read; robots.txt disallows every conventional path; a candidate or `Policy:` link, or the meta-refresh target of a `Policy:`, candidate or conventional-path page, was disallowed by robots.txt; a candidate or `Policy:` link, or the meta-refresh target of a `Policy:` or candidate page, was never requested (a request limit was reached, or its site had opted out or answered 429), or a planned page's record was removed, or the page itself withheld, because its site opted out after the crawl; a candidate, `Policy:`, conventional-path or meta-refresh-target request ended without a measurement (it failed, was refused, or ended with a status other than 200 or a 4xx), and no record of that address in either pass is a measurement; a stored page could not be read (a PDF without text, non-text other than an image, a page with fewer than 200 visible characters, or a page cut at the size limit in which nothing in the part kept matches the page pattern list or mentions `security.txt`), unless another stored copy of that address was read; a page was not judged; the homepage was cut at the size limit; the homepage had fewer than 200 visible characters, so no links could be read from it.
- no: the look was complete and found none.

Judged pages count from either pass, including a pass that ended blocked after reading them: a policy page from such a pass makes the value yes, and an unread or unjudged page from it makes the look incomplete, while the other reasons above come only from the passes that measured the domain.

A planned page counts as requested, and as read, when a homepage, `Policy:`, candidate or conventional-path request of the listed domain ended at its address with an answer, whichever address that request asked for (section 5).

Conventional paths, and the meta-refresh targets of conventional-path pages, that were never requested (the request limit was reached or, for a refresh target on another site, that site had opted out or answered 429) do not make the look incomplete; those domains are counted separately. A conventional path or refresh target whose record a purge removed (section 5) is not counted as cut: it makes the look incomplete, as a skipped page does.

### 6.7 Outside listings

Matched by registrable domain, with no contact with the organization, against snapshots pinned in `data/reference/registries/registries.meta.json`: the disclose.io database; in-scope web assets of public programmes on HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti and YesWeHack; the CVE Numbering Authority list. A disclose.io entry lists the registrable domains of its policy URL, security.txt URL and contact email. A platform programme lists its in-scope assets of web-like types. A CNA entry lists every web or email address in its contact, disclosure-policy and advisory fields. A domain is matched by registrable domain to these. A domain is listed when its listed domain, or (for a measured domain) the registrable domain of its final site, matches; which one matched is recorded. A link to a disclosure platform with a non-empty path counts as a listing on that platform only when one of these holds: the domain is already in that platform's registry scope; the link's text matches the page pattern list (section 6.1); or its first path segment contains the domain label of the listed domain or of the final site, or one of the organization's name words. The link may be on the homepage the domain was measured from (section 6.2), or given as a `Policy:` URL in the chosen `security.txt`.

### 6.8 First-seen dates

For domains with the main outcome, the earliest Internet Archive capture, with status 200 and a plain-text type, of the file at the host and path where it was found. This is a secondary, supplementary analysis (research question 5), reported apart from the primary results.

## 7. Outcomes

- Main: a `security.txt` classified valid or valid with problems. A forbidden `security.txt` (section 6.4) counts as none.
- Strict: a `security.txt` classified valid.
- Broad (the paper's any-channel outcome): the main outcome, or a policy page, or an outside listing. When none of these holds and either the `security.txt` class or the policy-page value is unmeasured, the broad outcome is unmeasured, not negative. Every outcome, including the listing, is defined only for measured domains (the sensitivity analysis in section 8 adds a registry listing of the listed domain for blocked and unreachable ones). Domains with an unmeasured value are left out of that share's denominator.

## 8. Analysis

- Shares with exact (Clopper-Pearson) 95% confidence intervals, by sector, per domain. The domain is the unit of inference. For hospitals, per-hospital and bed-weighted shares are also given, without intervals, because hospitals on one domain share an outcome. A bed-weighted share counts each hospital by its beds and leaves out hospitals whose beds are unknown (not a positive number in the HIFLD record); the number left out is given beside each bed-weighted share.
- Paired comparison of `cms-hpt.txt` and `security.txt` on hospital domains where both were measured (a file classified forbidden counts as measured, and as no file), with the exact McNemar test. A domain counts as having `security.txt` by the main outcome (valid or valid with problems), and as having `cms-hpt.txt` when the file is present and valid (section 6.5); a present file that is not valid counts as no file in the test, and the share of domains with any `cms-hpt.txt`, valid or not, is given beside it.
- Hospital characteristics, descriptive only: (a) system size over all hospital domains with exact intervals and a Fisher exact test of single-hospital against multi-hospital domains, (b) type, ownership, beds and rural location among single-hospital domains with exact intervals, and (c) per-hospital shares by characteristic without intervals. No regression model is fitted. A supplementary table, computed from `data/frames/hospitals.csv` alone and not by `noroshi analyze`, compares the 3,693 hospitals kept in the frame with the 3,838 eligible hospitals dropped at steps 4 to 6 of section 4.1 (no website listed, a website that cannot be read, or a website that belongs to someone else), by type, ownership and beds (unknown beds as their own category). The characteristics table uses the broad outcome over measured hospital domains whose broad value is known, grouped by system size (1, 2-5, 6-20, more than 20), type (general acute care, critical access, psychiatric, children, long term care, rehabilitation, other), ownership (non-profit, proprietary, non-federal government, unknown), beds (under 100, 100-299, 300 or more, unknown) and rural location (yes, no, unknown).
- Sensitivity (`tableS1_sensitivity`): the shares of the main share table (main, strict, policy page, listing and broad, per domain by sector, and for hospitals per hospital and bed-weighted) are repeated over every domain in the frame other than opted-out and uncrawled ones, with any value that could not be measured counted as lacking a channel. This covers both a domain that could not be measured and a single unmeasured outcome. A registry listing of the listed domain still counts toward listing and broad for a blocked or unreachable domain, though not for a mismatch, because a listing needs no crawl. Under the same rule, shares are also given counting each distinct final site once (positive when any listed domain leading to it is), since several listed domains can lead to one site. The characteristics, quality, paired and federal tables are not repeated.
- A quality table gives, among domains with the main outcome, the contact types, the state of the Expires field, signed files, files served as `text/plain` and parser agreement, and among invalid files the share whose only contacts were lenient or malformed. Among domains with a policy page, the share whose policy promises not to pursue good-faith reporters is also given, per sector with an exact interval. Figures show the main and broad shares by sector and, as a supplementary figure, the cumulative first-seen dates (section 6.8).
- The flow table reports, per sector: domains in the frame and by status (opted out, not crawled, blocked, unreachable, mismatch, measured); mismatch domains by reason (a crawl test, or the identity check's `other` or `unclear`); blocked domains that were rate-limited (the rest were refused); in the hospital row, unreachable domains whose last archived homepage capture redirected to another registrable domain (section 6.3); measured domains measured at another registrable domain (section 6.3); measured domains whose passes disagree (section 6.2); measured domains whose own site saw the browser signature, and those whose site was switched to it for the rest of a pass; measured domains with a file classified forbidden (section 6.4); measured domains whose policy-page value is unmeasured, in total and by each reason; measured domains with conventional paths cut by the request limit; requests sent, including those cut off by a crash or outage; and the number of domains that appear in more than one table. Its all-sectors row counts each domain once, and also gives the requests that had been sent to domains whose records were later deleted on opt-out.
- The HHS OCR breach portal is not used. A linkage of hospitals to breach history was not built: matching hospitals to breach reports by name is error-prone, and the research questions do not need it.
- Federal control check, descriptive and with no threshold: the broad share among measured federal domains whose broad value is known, counted per listed domain and per distinct final site (`tableS3_federal_control`; a final site is positive when any listed domain leading to it is). Every measured federal domain without any channel is examined by hand against its stored pages, and the reason is reported. A federal homepage that answers 200 with a page saying that the page does not exist counts as a no, because domains listed only in the federal table skip the identity check; this is a known limit.
- Agreement: `tableS2_agreement` gives, before any tie-break and with opted-out domains left out, the number of units, the share on which the two raters agreed and Cohen's kappa, for each of the judges' three questions (over the distinct page texts both judges answered) and for the identity raters' answer (over the decision derive used for each domain decided `own`, `other` or `unclear`, which is the one for the homepage the domain was measured from). Raw agreement is always shown beside kappa, and kappa is given without an interval. When both raters gave the same single answer to every unit, kappa is not defined and its cell is left empty. When one rater, or each of two raters who differ, gave the same answer every time, kappa is 0 and the table's note says so. For the identity check the note also counts the answers, the tie-break model's included, recorded as `unclear` because their replies could not be read.

## 11. Use of AI

Claude models, run as coding agents under the author's direction, searched the literature, wrote and reviewed the code, ran the collection and analysis, and drafted the paper. Each step is recorded in `logs/ai-use-log.md`. Model calls that are part of the method (sections 4.5, 6.3 and 6.6) are logged in full.

How the calls are made. Every call goes through the Claude Code command-line client (`claude -p`, `src/noroshi/llm.py`) with the model named, a fixed system prompt, no tools (the web-search call of section 4.5 is the one exception), no settings files and no saved session. The study sets no temperature and no token limit, so the client's defaults apply. A call is cut off after 300 seconds. The models are those of `src/noroshi/config.py` and `src/noroshi/resolve.py`: `claude-opus-5-5` and `claude-sonnet-5-5` judge pages, with `claude-fable-5-2` as their tie-break; `claude-sonnet-5-5` and `claude-haiku-5-5` check identity, with `claude-opus-5-5` as their tie-break; `claude-sonnet-5-5` and `claude-opus-5-5` resolve company websites, with `claude-sonnet-5-5` and web search for the names they do not agree on. Every call is written to `data/raw/llm_log.jsonl` with its purpose, model, prompt, reply, status, start time and duration in milliseconds.

## 12. Changes after the pilots

### 12.1 After the first pilot

The first pilot (150 domains, 30 per sector, one pass, 1,184 requests sent) was audited against its stored pages. Each entry says what the pilot showed and what the rule was changed to. Rules that a later entry changed again carry a pointer to it, and sections 5 to 8 state the rule as it stands. Crawler side:

- One spelling per URL. The pilot sent 10 identical requests twice, to two sites (`samhsa.gov` and `womenshealth.gov`), because a final address written with `:443` was not recognized as the same address without it; the repeats used up the site limit and cut the conventional paths of 5 domains. Now every URL is compared, requested and stored in one spelling, candidate links are de-duplicated by the same key as the set of requested addresses, and a repeated address is not requested (section 5).
- Other host form. 23 of 150 pilot homepages ended unreachable: 22 by a network error (TLS 10, DNS 7, connection 3, timeout 2) and one by status. Company and federal domains start at the bare name and hospitals at the listed host, and lookups of names only (no website contacted) showed several whose other `www.` form resolves. Now a network error at the start URL is followed by one request to the other host form, with the exceptions of section 6.1; there is still no plain-http fallback, and the outage stop counts domains, not requests (section 5).
- Browser signature. Three sites (`abbott.com`, `optum.com` reached from `changehealthcare.com`, and `insulet.com`) answered 403 at `/.well-known/security.txt` to both signatures, and each was switched to the browser signature for the rest of the pass (21 later requests and 3 retries) although its homepage had loaded with the identified one. Now a site is switched only when the browser retry got an answer (section 5). `fortwashingtonmc.org` ended blocked because its browser retry was redirected to a second site that had not refused yet, so that hop went out with the identified signature and was refused; the per-hop rule and the single retry are unchanged, and the pattern is counted in the second pilot.
- Forbidden files. The same three files were recorded as unmeasured, which made the main outcome of 4 domains (6 sector rows) unmeasured. Now a `security.txt` or `cms-hpt.txt` refused with 401 or 403 to both signatures, with no challenge page in either answer, on a site whose homepage was answered, is forbidden and is not retried in pass 2 (sections 6.4 and 6.5); a challenge page keeps the file unmeasured. The second pilot narrowed this rule (12.2, K5).
- Challenge pages. `intuitive.com` was measured with no page read: its homepage (212 bytes, status 200) held only an Incapsula script, and five conventional paths returned the same page. Incapsula also appends the same script to ordinary pages, such as that site's 404 `security.txt` pages, so the script counts only on a status 200 page with fewer than 200 visible characters. The 7 Akamai block pages in the pilot write their error address with HTML entities, so the plain marker matched none of them (all 7 were refused by status anyway); the entity form is now a marker (section 5).
- Crawl-time mismatch. The pilot ended 6 domains mismatch: 4 were the company's own homepage listed under another legal name (`predilife.com`, `videa.ai`, `crystalpm.com`, `revolutionehr.com`) and 2 were acquisitions decided by whether the acquirer's page held a listed word (`sjm.com`, `hillrom.com`). Five measured sites were not the organization's working site (`e-mercy.com` parked, `greenehealth.org` a dead host, `wvuniversityhealthcare.com` and `getqardio.com` taken over by others, `intuitive.com` a bot challenge). Now the crawl ends a domain mismatch only for the three tests of section 6.3: a listed final site, a for-sale or parked marker, or a homepage that ended on `ww<digits>.<listed domain>` (`e-mercy.com` ended on `ww547.e-mercy.com`) by a redirect or a followed meta refresh. The name-word comparison is no longer part of the crawl.
- Candidate links. The pilot sent 5 candidate-link requests across 117 measured domains. Among 83 domains with no policy page found, the audit found 3 definite misses (`changehealthcare.com`, whose Optum page says "Vulnerability report"; `vitalimages.com`, whose Canon page says "Coordinated Vulnerability Reporting"; `eppendorf.com`, "Cybersecurity") and about 9 likely ones ("Cybersecurity" and "Trust Center" links). Now links come in a specific and a general tier (section 6.1), at most 3 in all.
- Meta refresh. The `Policy:` address of `va.gov` answered 200 with a 654-byte page of 8 visible characters whose only content was a meta refresh to another host. It was not followed, and the domain's policy page was recorded as `no`. Now a short meta refresh on a nearly empty homepage or policy, candidate or conventional-path page is followed once (section 6.1).
- Homepage size. 4 of 123 pilot homepages (3%) were cut at 1 MiB (`tiogahealth.org`, `wchs-wy.org`, `miracare.com`, `modmed.com`), and each made the policy look unmeasured. Now a homepage is read up to 4,194,304 bytes and every other response up to 1,048,576 (section 5).
- Error text. A failed request kept only `tls`, `dns`, `connect` or `timeout`, so the audit could not tell a certificate that does not cover the bare name from an expired one. Now each failed attempt also stores the exception's class and message (cut to 300 characters) and the address of the hop it failed at.
- Expires. A value with no UTC offset was read as UTC, which is looser than RFC 3339. Now it is invalid and the file is valid with problems when its contact is valid (section 6.4). The pilot's files with an Expires value all carry an offset, so no pilot class changes.
- Pass 2 selection. A measured domain whose planned core file had no record (a request limit was reached before it was sent) was reported unmeasured but never retried. Now it is retried in pass 2 (section 6.2).

Analysis side:

- Judge prompt. The prompt ended with a filled-in example reply, and `claude-opus-5-5` returned exactly that reply (yes, yes, no) for 15 of the 18 distinct pages it judged and said yes to the first two questions for all 18, so the two judges' kappa on those questions was 0.00. The page's address was also in the prompt: six identical pages differed only in it, and five were judged yes and one no. Now the prompt carries no example answer and no address, and the address is written beside the call in the model-call log (section 6.6).
- Pages the judge cannot use. Six conventional paths at `grants.gov` answered 200 with one 999-character shell, and five were recorded as policy pages. Two stored copies of one policy with the same text and different bytes were judged twice and one judge gave different safe-harbor answers. The `Policy:` page of `va.gov` (8 visible characters, a meta refresh) and a cut page at `modmed.com` (36 visible characters) were counted as clear negatives, as were script shells and challenge pages at the conventional paths of `birthcontrol.gov`, `digidms.com` and `intuitive.com`. Now a page is set aside before judging as a catch-all page, a followed refresh or an unreadable page, one verdict is kept per distinct visible text, an unreadable page makes the look incomplete, and so does a homepage with fewer than 200 visible characters, whose links could not be read (sections 6.3 and 6.6).
- Prefilter wording. The two wordings of the missed links above, "Vulnerability report" and "Coordinated Vulnerability Reporting", were not in the page pattern list that drops clear negatives, so a policy page using only them would have been dropped. Now the list has `coordinated vulnerability` and `vulnerability or vulnerabilities report` beside the specific tier's other wording, and nothing from the general tier (section 6.1).
- Identity check. Five measured pilot sites were not the organization's working website (`e-mercy.com` parked, `greenehealth.org` a dead host, `wvuniversityhealthcare.com` and `getqardio.com` taken over by others, `intuitive.com` a bot challenge) and were counted as organizations with no channel; four company sites that were the company's own homepage had been thrown out by the word match; and acquisitions were decided by whether the acquirer's page held a listed word. Now two models, with a third for a disagreement, decide after the crawl whether each measured homepage is the organization's working website or that of its present owner (section 6.3), and the main analysis waits for every decision.
- What derive reports of the crawler's rules. The three files refused to both signatures were recorded as unmeasured, and the `browser` count (13 measured domains) included three sites switched to the browser signature after a refusal at one path. Now the class `forbidden`, the number of domains with a forbidden file, and the sites switched to the browser signature are reported, apart from the sites that saw it once. A file read at another registrable domain (the `security.txt` of `bridgeinteract.io`, the `cms-hpt.txt` of two hospitals) is flagged. A measured domain's homepage is read through one definition, the last homepage request of the latest pass that measured it, for the final site, the links and the identity check (sections 6.2 to 6.5).
- Tables the protocol promised. The agreement statistic, the passes that disagree, the archived redirects of unreachable hospitals, the safe-harbor share among policy pages, the domains measured at another registrable domain, the mismatches by reason and the parser disagreements had no code behind them. Now each has a table, a flow-table count or a file (section 8). The pilot's kappa of 0.00 stood beside a judge that always said yes; kappa is now shown with raw agreement and left empty when both raters gave the same single answer to every unit. The federal control is counted per listed domain and per final site, because 8 of the 24 measured federal pilot rows land on a site that is itself in the frame (20 of 24 by listed domain, 16 of 20 by final site).
- Opt-out purge. The review of the purge found that a page deleted from another domain's look was counted as cut by the request limit, so that domain's policy page became `no`; that the markers for opted-out domains were written apart from the deletions; that a purge erased another site's 429 or refusal that a resumed pass still needs; that the browser-signature mark and the error text of the opted-out site stayed in the store; and that the derived tables could be analyzed after a purge had counted its requests twice. Now a purge leaves a trace for the look, writes the deletions and markers in one transaction, removes the mark, and the analysis refuses while the purge log is newer than the derived tables (section 5). The code review that followed changed the rest of the rule: a purge deletes every record that names the opted-out site, instead of emptying the address in the records of other sites, and keeps what a resumed pass needs as counts without any address; a purge stopped at any point can be finished by running it again; and the other commands refuse while a purge is pending, and a purge and the model runs exclude each other (section 5). After a purge nothing the study keeps holds an address of the opted-out site, with the exceptions section 5 names.
- Second pilot. A second pilot draws its sample after removing the first pilot's domains, so it does not visit them again (section 5).

### 12.2 After the second pilot

The second pilot (150 sector rows on 148 distinct domains, one pass, 1,365 requests sent and 1,383 records stored, run at commit `8a7dc5b`) was audited against its stored pages by two readers, one for the crawl and one for the classification. The crawl kept every public promise (no violation in the 1,383 records: the limits per domain and per site, the 2-second gap, GET only, the redirect limit, the browser signature only after a refusal, robots.txt, and nothing sent after a 429), and no policy-page judgement was wrong. The rules below misfired. Each entry gives what the audit found and the rule now. Crawler side:

- K1, a refused site and its browser retry. `hhs.gov` received 6 refused requests from 6 federal domains in 11 seconds, 5 of them for the same address, before the switch to the browser signature took effect: the switch was recorded only when a whole retry had ended, and requests of other domains already queued for the site went out with the study's signature. They used 6 of its 12 non-homepage requests and cut the conventional paths of three domains (`globalhealth.gov`, `psc.gov`, `selectagents.gov`). Most federal domains link the same HHS page (about 17 of the 23 measured federal domains), and the main pass keeps 20 domains in flight, so the refusals could use up the limit before the page was read. Now other requests to a site wait while the browser retry of a refused request is pending (section 5).
- K2, an address already answered. The `Policy:` address of `qmenta.com` redirected to a page that a candidate then requested again, and a menu link `href="#"` on `infinx.com`, whose homepage had ended at another spelling of its own address, requested the homepage again as a candidate (2 domains, 2 requests). Now an address at which a homepage, `Policy:`, candidate or conventional-path request of the listed domain already got an answer is not requested again (section 5), and a page counts as read through the page request that ended at its address (section 6.6). A first version also counted answers to `robots.txt`, `security.txt` and `cms-hpt.txt` requests, so a page first reached through such a redirect was never requested as a page and never judged; fix round 4 removed them.
- K3, `<base href>`. The homepage of `ypsomed.com` carried `<base href="https://www.ypsomed.com/">` and a relative link; the link was resolved against the page's final address, became `/en/en/legal-information/vulnerability-disclosure` and answered 404 (1 of 4 measured homepages with a `<base>`; one request wasted, no outcome changed). Now links are resolved against the base (section 6.1).
- K4, wording. The `/security` page of `roche.com` read "Vulnerability Rewards Program" and gave a contact address, and was never shown to a model, because the page pattern list had no "rewards" wording, so its policy page read `no` (2 rows). Now "vulnerability rewards" is in both wording lists (section 6.1).
- K5, challenge pages. All six answers at the three files of `rimrock.org` were CleanTalk's "SpamFireWall is checking your browser" page, which carries none of the fixed strings, so the files were classed forbidden and counted as no file (1 of 18 measured hospital rows; the hospital main and strict denominators were 18 instead of 17). Now `spamfirewall` is a fixed string (section 5), and a file is forbidden only when both answers are plain, with no script in either (section 6.4). Of the stored 401 and 403 answers at file locations, 6 of the second pilot's 24 contain a script (all of them `rimrock.org`'s) and none of the first pilot's 6; replayed on both stored pilots, the second pilot's forbidden locations fall from 11 on 7 domains to 8 on 6, and the first pilot's three forbidden files stay.

Classification side:

- K6, identity prompt. Of the 8 domains the identity check judged `unclear` in the second pilot, 3 were the organization's own working sites that show little text without scripts (`corvista.com`, a script shell; `caretracker.com` and `inmediata.com`, script redirects), and they ended `mismatch` and left every denominator (2 of 28 EHR rows). The prompt named such a page as its example of `unclear`. Now two bullets of the prompt are reworded: `own` counts a page with little text of its own when its title or meta tags name the organization or its product, and `unclear` means that nothing on the page shows whose website it is (section 6.3).
- K7, pages that count as read. Two domains were left `unmeasured` by pages that plainly are not policy pages: `/security` of `modulemd.com` redirected to a 1,359-byte JPEG (`non_text`), and the six conventional paths of `ossur.com` redirected to its own homepage and were cut at 1 MiB (`unreadable`). Now an image answer and a candidate or conventional-path page that ends at the homepage's own address count as read and are not policy pages (section 6.6). Applied to the stored second pilot without a model call, `ossur.com` and `modulemd.com` change from `unmeasured` to `no`; `infinx.com` (a Trust Center page cut at 1 MiB with no visible text) and `bmc-medical.com` (a 200 page reading "404" at every path) stay `unmeasured`.

Code review after the pilot's crawl (a review of the analysis-side changes and two re-reviews of its fixes) also changed the code. These changes were not pilot findings. Their results are the purge and its locks (section 5, 12.1), the handling of an unreadable identity reply and of the page title (section 6.3), kappa (section 8), the analysis guards (section 6.2), the rule that a stop signal leaves a model run's calls in flight to finish and starts none (sections 6.3 and 6.6; a `pdftotext` ended by a signal after a stop was asked writes no verdict, and SIGHUP counts as a signal), and a frame check that every listed domain is its own registrable domain (section 4.6).

Unchanged: the browser retry that is redirected to a second site that had not refused (`fortwashingtonmc.org`, first pilot) did not recur in the second pilot (0 blocked chains in 150 sector rows; 1 of 60 hospital rows over both pilots), and the rule stands as built.

The changes of 12.2 were verified on the stored pilot pages and in tests against local servers only, and went into the main pass without a further pilot.

## 13. Known limits

These limits are known before collection. They are reported with the results.

- The policy-page measure is a lower bound. A link found on a candidate or conventional-path page is not followed (section 6.1). In the second pilot's audit the measure found 15 of 18 device and ICSMA companies that publish a disclosure policy. The three it missed: `roche.com`, whose page the pattern list dropped (corrected by K4, section 12.2), `bostonscientific.com`, whose product-security page links its policy one step further, and `gehealthcare.com`, whose policy is linked from no page that was requested. None of the three changed the any-channel outcome, because each has a registry listing or a `security.txt`. On the 18 measured hospital homepages the audit found no disclosure link.
- A hospital domain may lawfully lack `cms-hpt.txt`: the rule places the file at the root of whichever site hosts the machine-readable price file, which may be another domain (section 6.5). The comparison with `security.txt` is on the listed domain only.
- The outside-listing measure is a lower bound. Platform directories list only the programs that chose to be listed, so a program that is private or unlisted is not found (section 6.7).
- Federal error homepages count as no (section 8).
- A file on a site switched to the browser signature is never classed forbidden (section 6.4).
- Pass 2 can re-crawl domains whose outcome cannot change, for example a measured domain that already has a judged policy page but had a failed page request, or a domain the identity check later ends as a mismatch (section 6.2). The extra requests stay within the limits of section 5.
- The hospital frame covers only hospitals with a listed website: 3,597 of 7,531 eligible hospitals (47.8%) list none (section 4.1).
- Pages other than the homepage are read to 1,048,576 bytes; a longer page can be unreadable and leave the look unmeasured (section 6.6).
- A real policy served at two conventional paths as aliases, with the same text and no redirect, is set aside as a catch-all page (section 6.6).
- Two spellings of one page, for example paths that differ by a locale segment or a query string, can take two of the three candidate slots (section 6.1).
- The identity check can label a parked page `unclear` instead of `other`; both end as a mismatch, so the flow table's `identity_unclear` count includes some parked pages (section 6.3).
